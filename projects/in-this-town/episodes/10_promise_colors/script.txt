In this city, every time you break a promise, one of your colors leaves your body. Break seven, and you turn completely gray, and nobody can see you ever again. You see, red goes first, out of your lips. Then the orange in your hair, then the yellow in your skin. Mr. Dale promised a refund and never paid it, now his tie is gray. The mayor promised us a new bridge, and her eyes have no green left. Because of this, nobody here says promise. We say probably. The bakery is probably open. I'll probably call you back. My name is Nia, and I have broken six promises. The only color I have left is the blue in my eyes. But one day, I promised my little brother Theo I would walk him home from school at three. I was late. When the clock tower rang three, I felt it. The blue started dripping out of my eyes like paint. By midnight it would be gone, and so would I. And Theo wasn't at school. He had gone looking for me. I ran under the old gray bridge, where the faded people live. An old woman I could only see in the puddles grabbed my wrist. She pushed a piece of white chalk into my hand. A broken promise can only be fixed by keeping a bigger one, she said. And whatever you do, never promise the gray man in the clock tower. The city was losing color street by street. Doors turned gray, then windows, then the water. My own reflection was gone from the canal. Gray hands waved from every bridge, and none of my footsteps made a sound. At the top of the clock tower, I found Theo, and behind him stood the gray man, tying ribbons of stolen color to the bell rope. Then he turned around, and I knew his face. It was Dad. Five years ago, he promised he'd be home for dinner, and he never came back. We thought he left. He never left. Nobody could see him. Promise you'll stay, Dad whispered. I didn't. Instead, I grabbed Theo's hand and said, I'm walking you home now. And I did. Every step, a little blue came back into my eyes. But Dad laughed softly. You think I'm the only one up here? The whole tower was full of gray people, hundreds of them, all reaching for us. So I took out the chalk, and on the giant clock face where the whole city could see it, I wrote, I promise to bring everyone home. Then the bell rang midnight. The ribbons snapped, and every color in the city flew back where it belonged. In the morning, the bridges were brighter than ever. Dad was at the dinner table, and people started saying promise again. Then Theo looked at me and said, promise you'll never break another one. I opened my mouth, and my lips went a tiny bit gray.
